## Specialized knowledge for construction and contractor accounting

Contractor accounting breaks most general practitioners. Job costing has to tie actual labor, materials, and equipment costs back to individual projects, not just the company as a whole. Work-in-progress schedules have to reflect percentage-of-completion accurately enough to satisfy bonding companies and lenders. Retainage, multi-state tax exposure, and equipment depreciation schedules add more layers most bookkeepers never learn to manage correctly.

BussCPA builds job costing systems and WIP schedules that hold up under bonding company review, not just internal management reports. Contractors get tax planning that accounts for equipment purchases, Section 179 deductions, and the timing quirks that come with long-term contracts. This is construction accounting handled by people who know what a bonding agent actually asks for.

## Farm and agriculture accounting expertise unique to South Dakota

Agriculture operates on a different clock than most industries, and South Dakota's farm economy makes that even more pronounced. Payroll needs shift with planting and harvest seasons. Cash accounting elections, income averaging, and equipment depreciation all carry rules that don't apply to a retail shop or a law office. Get them wrong and a farm family can lose thousands to avoidable tax exposure or miss a payroll deadline during the busiest week of the year.

BussCPA treats agricultural clients as a distinct practice area, not a variation on general small business accounting. That means seasonal payroll structured around actual cash flow, tax planning built around commodity price swings, and farm-specific deductions applied correctly the first time. For an agriculture enterprise near Hartford SD, that specificity is the difference between a tax preparer and an advisor who understands the operation.
