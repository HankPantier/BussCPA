## Track your federal and state refund in minutes

Most Hartford filers only need to check one place: the IRS Where's My Refund tool for their federal return. South Dakota does not collect a personal income tax, so unless you worked or filed in another state this year, there's no separate state refund to track down. If you or a spouse earned wages in Minnesota, Iowa, or elsewhere, that state's Department of Revenue site will have its own status page, and the wait times rarely match.

We're not here to replace the IRS tracker. We're here because the process trips people up, especially when a return includes amended filings, business income, or multiple states. If your status page says "still processing" longer than expected, that's usually a sign something in the return needs a second look. Our [personal income tax](/services/personal-income-tax) team can pull your filing details and tell you what's actually happening.

## What you need before you check your status

Before you sit down at the IRS site, have three things ready: your Social Security number or ITIN, your filing status, and the exact refund amount shown on your return, down to the dollar. A rounded number or a typo in any of these will kick you out of the tool, and that's the most common reason Hartford filers get stuck.

- Social Security number or ITIN for the primary filer
- Filing status exactly as it appears on the return (single, married filing jointly, etc.)
- The exact whole-dollar refund amount from your filed return
- Your prior-year return on hand if you filed an amended 1040-X
- Patience for the 24 to 48 hour lag after e-filing before the tool updates

If you don't have a copy of your return handy, we keep digital records for every BussCPA client, so a quick call gets you the numbers in minutes, not days.

## Why refunds get delayed for local businesses and individuals

A delayed refund almost always traces back to something specific in the return, not bad luck. For construction contractors, the culprit is often depreciation schedules or Section 179 deductions on equipment purchases that the IRS flags for manual review. A single miscoded asset can add weeks to processing.

Agriculture clients run into a different problem: seasonal income and payroll timing. Farm operations that pay employees unevenly through the year, or that report income from grain sales concentrated in a few months, sometimes trigger IRS matching checks against 1099s and W-2s that arrive later than the return itself. That mismatch stalls the refund until the numbers reconcile.

For individuals, the usual suspects are itemized deductions that need documentation, amended returns still working through the 1040-X queue, or a refund offset because of past-due child support or state debt. None of these mean you did something wrong. They mean the return needs a human to confirm the details, and that's exactly the kind of work we do every week for households and business owners across Hartford.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Check Your Tax Refund Status | Buss CPA

**Q: Does South Dakota have a state income tax refund?**
A: No. South Dakota does not levy a personal income tax, so residents only track a federal refund through the IRS unless they worked or filed a return in another state during the tax year, in which case that state's Department of Revenue handles the status check separately.

**Q: How long does it take to get a federal tax refund?**
A: Most e-filed federal returns with direct deposit process within 21 days. Paper returns, amended 1040-X filings, or returns flagged for review, common for construction depreciation or agriculture payroll mismatches, can take six weeks or longer to resolve.

**Q: What information do I need to check my refund status?**
A: You'll need your Social Security number or ITIN, your exact filing status, and the precise whole-dollar refund amount from your filed return. A mismatch on any of these three details is the most common reason the IRS tool won't return results.

**Q: Why is my refund delayed if I'm a contractor or farm owner?**
A: Construction returns often get flagged over equipment depreciation or Section 179 deductions, while agriculture returns can stall when 1099s from grain buyers or co-ops arrive after the return is filed, creating a mismatch the IRS needs to reconcile before releasing funds.

A: We can review your filed return, confirm the numbers the IRS is matching against, and follow up directly with the agency if your refund is delayed or reduced. Clients get real-time digital access to their records, so we're never starting from a blank file.
