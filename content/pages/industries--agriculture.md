## Accounting built around the realities of farm operations

Farming doesn't run on a calendar quarter, and neither should your books. Crop insurance proceeds land in one month, cattle sell in another, and payroll spikes hard during planting and harvest. Most CPA firms build their processes around retail or service businesses, then try to make agriculture fit the same mold. BussCPA does it the other way around.

From our office in Hartford, SD, we work with row crop operations, cattle producers, and diversified farms across the greater Sioux Falls area. We understand why a Schedule F return looks nothing like a standard business filing, why seasonal labor changes payroll obligations month to month, and why a bad year for commodity prices can turn a profitable operation into a cash-flow puzzle. That's the accounting our farm clients get: built for how agriculture actually operates, not adapted from a template designed for someone else's business.

## Payroll and tax challenges unique to agriculture

Generic accounting firms often miss the details that matter most to a farm's bottom line. We don't.

- Seasonal labor and farm payroll rules: hired hands during planting and harvest trigger different withholding, unemployment insurance, and reporting requirements than year-round employees.
- Equipment depreciation strategy: Section 179 deductions and bonus depreciation decisions on tractors, combines, and grain bins affect your tax bill for years, not just the current season.
- Commodity-driven income swings: a strong corn year followed by a drought year means income averaging and deferred grain contracts need real planning, not guesswork after the fact.
- Schedule F complexity: farm income and expense reporting has its own rules for prepaid supplies, crop insurance proceeds, and government payments that a standard business return doesn't address.

If your current accountant treats your operation like any other small business, you're probably leaving deductions and planning opportunities on the table.

## Outsourced accounting for crop and livestock operations

Our [outsourced accounting services](/services/outsourced-accounting) cover bookkeeping, financial statements, and payroll for crop and livestock operations under one fixed monthly fee. No surprise invoices during a year when margins are already tight.

We reconcile accounts, categorize input costs by field or herd, and produce financial statements that mean something to a lender or landlord, not just an IRS form. Because everything runs through secure digital workflows, you get real-time online access to your numbers whether you're in the cab of a combine or sitting down with your banker to talk operating lines. Livestock producers get the same structure: feed costs, breeding stock basis, and market timing tracked consistently through the year instead of reconstructed at tax time. It's accounting that keeps pace with a business that doesn't slow down for anyone's fiscal year.

## Farm tax planning and preparation done right

Farm tax preparation is reactive by default, filed once a year after the decisions that shaped the return are already made. We push planning earlier.

Through the year, we look at grain sale timing, prepaid expense elections, equipment purchase timing, and income averaging opportunities before December 31 instead of after. That means fewer surprises at filing time and fewer decisions made purely to avoid a tax bill instead of to grow the operation. Our [personal income tax](/services/personal-income-tax) work extends the same approach to farm owners filing individually, coordinating the farm's Schedule F with household income, retirement contributions, and estate considerations so nothing gets planned in isolation. Farm tax planning done a few months ahead of the deadline beats farm tax preparation done the week of it, every time.
