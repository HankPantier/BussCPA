## Why law firms have unique accounting and tax needs

Law firm accounting carries risks a standard small-business CPA rarely encounters. Client funds, entity structure, and partner compensation all require specific handling, and mistakes here aren't just costly, they can be an ethics issue.

- **Trust accounting and IOLTA compliance**, client funds must stay separate from operating funds, with reconciliations that hold up to bar association scrutiny
- **Retainer and client cost tracking**, accurate ledgers for advanced fees and reimbursable expenses, tied to individual matters
- **Partner draws and distributions**, clear accounting for how profits move to equity partners versus staff attorney compensation
- **Entity structuring**, guidance on PLLC, partnership, or S-corp elections that affect self-employment tax and liability exposure
- **Payroll for mixed staff**, associates, paralegals, and of-counsel attorneys often sit on different pay and benefit structures

## Tax planning and preparation for attorneys and firms

A law firm's tax picture changes depending on entity type, and the deadlines don't wait for trial prep. Whether a firm operates as a partnership, PLLC, or S-corp, entity-level filings require planning well before the return is due, not a scramble in March.

We prepare and file entity returns alongside individual returns for equity partners and solo attorneys, so tax obligations at the firm and personal level stay coordinated instead of siloed. Deduction planning covers continuing legal education, bar dues, malpractice insurance, and office expenses that generalist preparers sometimes miss entirely.

Quarterly estimated payments get calculated ahead of time, not guessed at after the fact. Attorneys practicing in South Dakota also avoid state income tax on personal returns, but firm-level and multi-state filings for attorneys licensed elsewhere still need attention. Personal income tax services extend to a firm's partners individually, keeping both sides of the return aligned.

## CFO-level advisory for growing law firms

Bookkeeping tells you what happened. Advisory tells you what to do next. As firms add attorneys or open a second office, decisions about partner compensation structures, cash flow timing, and reinvestment start to matter more than last month's ledger.

Our advisory services give managing partners the numbers behind those decisions: cash flow forecasts that account for uneven case revenue, compensation models that keep equity partners aligned, and profitability breakdowns by practice area. Firms considering a lateral hire or a new office lease get a clearer picture of what the firm can actually absorb before signing anything.

This is the layer most law firms skip until a cash crunch forces the conversation. Building it in earlier costs less than fixing it later.
